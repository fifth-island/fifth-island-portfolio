.research

DCASE 2026 Workshop · co-first author

What can a coda tell us?

Sperm whales talk in codas: short bursts of clicks. Their rhythm (the gaps between clicks) and their spectrum (the texture of each click) may carry different information. We built DCCE, a dual-channel contrastive encoder that reads the two separately before fusing them: a GRU over inter-click intervals and a CNN over log-mel spectrograms.

On 1,383 codas from the Dominica Sperm Whale Project, DCCE beat frozen bioacoustic foundation models (WhAM, AVES, BirdNET) at coda-type classification, and date-held-out tests exposed how much recording conditions inflate random-split scores.

FORM · my training journal · since Sep 2026

I treat my own training as a dataset.

Every CrossFit session I do goes into FORM, a training journal I built and use daily. It doesn't just store workouts: it labels them, measures them, ranks each one against my recent baseline, grades every week out of 100, and writes next week's plan from whatever the data says was missing.

It's the same way I approach engineering problems: decide what counts, measure it honestly, and let the evidence pick the next move.

Open the live dashboard ↗ Weekly reviews ↗ Session rankings ↗
  1. 01Capture

    Paste the gym's whiteboard; a deterministic parser drafts the session. Nothing counts until I confirm it.

  2. 02Label

    Each of 76 movements maps to the muscles it trains (direct, primary, secondary) and one of 10 movement patterns.

  3. 03Measure

    Exposure points per muscle (2 / 1 / 0.5, capped per session) and session demand from RPE × minutes, or a labeled estimate.

  4. 04Rank

    Every session against the median of the ten before it; every lift against my heaviest earlier entry.

  5. 05Review

    A weekly score out of 100, with what went well, what to improve and which muscles or patterns were missing.

  6. 06Plan

    Next week's accessory blocks are generated from the deficits, then checked for follow-through.

  7. 07Model

    Next: once enough clean weeks exist, move from rules to statistics and machine learning.

rules the data has to follow

  • unknown loads stay null, never guessed
  • unconfirmed or skipped entries never count
  • a baseline needs at least 3 earlier sessions
  • plans made after the fact are labeled hindsight
  • exposure points are not called hypertrophy sets

where it's going

The rules are deliberately simple so the data stays trustworthy while it grows. With a few months of sessions, the plan is to test what actually drives a strong week, fit progression curves for each lift, estimate fatigue and readiness from session RPE, relate training exposure to body-composition scans, and eventually let a model suggest the next session.
