I started as a graphic designer, became a software engineer, and found my edge in data and AI.

I grew up in Goiânia, Brazil, where a technical design program at SENAI taught me to care about how things look and feel. Today I'm a software engineer at Dimenso, building the AI behind a lab copilot: smart glasses and a voice agent that follow scientists through their protocols, and the systems that turn what they capture into clean, trustworthy records. On the side, I co-wrote a paper on how sperm whales encode identity in their clicks, mentor robotics teams back home, and build the apps I use to train for CrossFit.

.research

DCASE 2026 Workshop · co-first author

What can a coda tell us?

Sperm whales talk in codas: short bursts of clicks. Their rhythm (the gaps between clicks) and their spectrum (the texture of each click) may carry different information. We built DCCE, a dual-channel contrastive encoder that reads the two separately before fusing them: a GRU over inter-click intervals and a CNN over log-mel spectrograms.

On 1,383 codas from the Dominica Sperm Whale Project, DCCE beat frozen bioacoustic foundation models (WhAM, AVES, BirdNET) at coda-type classification, and date-held-out tests exposed how much recording conditions inflate random-split scores.
